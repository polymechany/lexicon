**Lexicon of Polymechany**

> Reference definitions for **polymechany**, **polymechan**, and **polymechanize** — the systemic condition in which adaptable interfaces collapse the coordination costs of disparate skill stacks.

### Etymology

From Greek *polymēchanos* (“of many devices,” “ingenious in contrivance”), an epithet applied in Homeric Greek to Odysseus for resourcefulness in means rather than breadth of learning; the abstract noun is *polymēchania* (πολυμηχανία). The [OED](https://www.oed.com/dictionary/polymechany_n) records the English noun *polymechany* as obsolete, with its only known use in the late 1500s (earliest evidence 1592, Gabriel Harvey, whose *Foure Letters* of that year use the word; [text in G. Gregory Smith, *Elizabethan Critical Essays*, vol. 2](https://archive.org/stream/elizabethancriti002smit/elizabethancriti002smit_djvu.txt)). It reappears as the title of a chapter on Harvey in Jessica Wolfe’s *Humanism, Machinery, and Renaissance Literature* (Cambridge University Press, 2004; Chapter 4, “The polymechany of Gabriel Harvey,” p. 125; [contents](https://assets.cambridge.org/97805211/23761/frontmatter/9780521123761_frontmatter.pdf)), where it is linked to Odyssean cunning and the study of mechanics. In those uses the word names a person’s practical ingenuity. This lexicon revives it with a different referent, a condition of a system in which interfaces carry that skill, and retains the root *mēchanē* (device, contrivance) to emphasize tool- and system-mediated capability over internalized knowledge.

### Definitions

#### Formal

- **Polymechany** (n.): The systemic condition in which adaptable interfaces collapse the coordination costs of disparate skill stacks, so that cross-domain execution from high-level intent becomes the ordinary unit of effective action rather than an exceptional or elite practice. Applies to a stated bounded environment (toolchain, workflow, team, or organization) that meets or exceeds the P1 threshold.
- **Polymechan** (n.): A practitioner (human or non-human) who, with respect to such an environment, supplies high-level intent and exercises residual judgment, including detecting failed absorption.
- **Polymechanize** (v.): To redesign an environment so that it absorbs technical friction and thereby lowers the barrier to cross-domain action.

The practitioner role is functional. A human or a non-human intelligence can occupy it; the term names the role, not the kind of occupant. The role is defined by its relation to the absorbing system, so that in the closure case the absorbing system itself can occupy it. See Locus of Intent, under Closure.

**Interface** has its ordinary sense: the means through which a practitioner works with a system, and so the point where the practitioner’s intent enters. The **absorbing system** is the system that takes on the work the practitioner would otherwise have to do: the interface together with whatever it uses to perform or coordinate the translation between domains, constraint work, and intermediate technical work (see absorption, under Distinctions). Constraint work means identifying, reconciling, and satisfying constraints across domains. Absorption is a relation between the practitioner and the absorbing system. Where a definition says that adaptable interfaces collapse coordination costs, the absorbing system is meant. A **skill stack** is the set of specialist skills and tools that a domain ordinarily requires.

#### Informal

- **Polymechany**: The condition in which adaptable systems collapse the coordination costs of disparate skill stacks, so that high-level intent can be enacted across domains without requiring internalized specialization in each, and without that work being pushed onto the user or hidden.
- **Polymechan**: The role that supplies that intent and exercises judgment over it, whether the role is occupied by an external agent or, where S is established under rule 3, by the system itself.
- **Polymechanize**: To change a workflow or set of tools so that greater polymechany can be achieved.

**Register.** The formal register is the default: a term used without a label is read formally. Any staged, measured, comparative, or forecast claim must be made in the formal register. The informal register is for ordinary-language and projective use and carries no stage, measurement, or test.

### Distinctions

Polymechany is a property of the surrounding system, not a personal trait. It is therefore distinct from:

- **Polymathy**, which names the personal acquisition and retention of knowledge across fields.
- **Interdisciplinarity or transdisciplinarity**, which name modes of inquiry or collaborative practice rather than a baseline technological condition under which an agent’s intent operates.
- **Generalism or jack-of-all-trades**, which describe personal breadth and carry no implication that coordination costs have been absorbed.

The defining shift is from mastery that must be held inside the practitioner to coordination costs that the absorbing system takes on. Absorption means the absorbing system performs or reliably coordinates the relevant translation, constraint work, and intermediate technical work. It does not include coordination burdens that, for the instance at hand, are merely deferred, shifted to the practitioner or another party, or concealed until failure. Here, coordination costs means the work and burden of translating, sequencing, and integrating across disparate skill stacks, not merely monetary expense. A reduction in immediate friction is not, by itself, evidence that coordination costs have been absorbed.

**Dependence.** Absorption at a given time rests on earlier work: tools built, standards set, capabilities trained, often by other parties. This lexicon names the condition at the time of use, namely who bears the live coordination cost of this intent in this environment. It makes no claim that this layer is independent of, or larger than, the earlier ones. It is a status concept, not a causal account: it does not claim that present absorption is the largest or most important contributor to what the environment accomplishes. Costs paid once upstream and reused across many tasks are not “deferred” or “transferred” in the sense of the absorption condition; burdens that return to the practitioner or a third party for this instance are.

Residual judgment by the practitioner includes detecting failed absorption, including by means of tests, audits, or cross-checks the environment supplies.

Polymechany is also distinct from nearby system-level concepts. Abstraction hides detail within a layer or domain; automation replaces specific tasks; modularity permits recombination across defined interfaces; low-code lowers entry barriers within a platform’s scope. Deskilling describes a possible effect on practitioners, not a condition of the system. In the formal register, polymechany names the absorption of cross-domain coordination costs such that high-level intent becomes the ordinary unit of action. Abstraction, automation, modularity, and low-code may contribute to it; none alone constitutes it.

### Ordinal Scale

The ordinal scale, including the P1 threshold and its stages, belongs to the formal register. It grades environments below P1 as well as above it; only P1 and above is a polymechany, and only under the formal definition. The informal register does not use the P1 gate.

Degrees of polymechany are graded along three dimensions:

- **Domain span**: the heterogeneity of skill stacks traversable from a single high-level intent, and the range of intents over which that traversal is available.
- **Friction absorption**: the fraction of translation, constraint work, and intermediate technical work performed by the absorbing system rather than by the practitioner.
- **Defaultness**: the extent to which cross-domain execution is the ordinary mode of action in the environment rather than an exceptional practice.

An environment is any bounded setting in which interfaces and practitioners operate together, such as a toolchain, workflow, team, or organization. Stage assignments are relative to the stated environment.

The scale describes the degree of absorption, not its desirability. Higher stages do not imply better outcomes, and “polymechanize” names an intervention, not an endorsement.

The three dimensions need not advance together. An environment may show high domain span with low reliability, high friction absorption inside a narrow cluster, or high defaultness without genuine cross-domain span. When the dimensions diverge, stage assignment is a matter of explicit judgment rather than a mechanical rule.

#### The P1 Threshold

The boundary between P0 and P1 marks the point at which an environment counts as a polymechany at all. An environment crosses it when all three of the following hold, each corresponding to one of the dimensions above:

1. **Span.** The environment supports execution across genuinely disparate skill stacks, meaning stacks that would ordinarily require separate specialist training, from a single high-level intent, rather than merely across multiple tools or tasks within one domain, and does so for intents that were not specified in advance, rather than only for a fixed set the environment was built to handle.
2. **Absorption.** The absorbing system reliably performs the relevant cross-domain translation and constraint work, with failures detectable by checks the environment supplies, and with no material coordination burden, for the instance at hand, merely deferred, concealed, or transferred to the practitioner or another party. Absorption is assessed on work actually performed for an intent; capability that has not been exercised is potential, not absorption.
3. **Defaultness.** This mode of cross-domain execution is the environment’s ordinary practice, rather than an exceptional, handcrafted, or specially arranged workflow.

Absorption is necessary for anything above P0, because it is what the term names. Span and defaultness without absorption describe practitioners who integrate across domains themselves, which is multi-tool practice or personal breadth, not polymechany. An environment is therefore **Late P0** when absorption is met together with either span or defaultness, but not both. Absorption alone, within one domain, is abstraction or automation and remains P0. The conditions are qualitative: what counts as “reliably,” “material,” or “disparate” remains a judgment until proxies exist.

#### Stages

- **P0 (Pre-polymechany)**: Interfaces accelerate work inside isolated disciplines. The practitioner remains the primary integrator. Outside the Late P0 band, domain span, friction absorption, and defaultness are all low. Environments without adaptable interfaces fall outside the scale.
  - **Late P0 (assistive)**: Absorption is met together with either span or defaultness, but not both. Two forms are typical: the absorbing system takes on substantial friction inside limited domain clusters, or for a fixed set of intents, where this is the default but the span is narrow; or the absorbing system reliably takes on cross-domain work in workflows that remain exceptional or specially arranged. In both, the practitioner continues to supply high-level intent, trade-off judgment, and frequent correction. Late P0 is a band within P0, not a separate stage, and it is not yet a polymechany.
- **P1 (Operational)**: All three threshold conditions are met within the stated environment. Cross-domain intent is the ordinary unit of work, absorption is reliable across the relevant stacks, and working this way is the default. The practitioner’s residual role is primarily semantic intent and final judgment, including detecting failed absorption. An environment at P1 or above is a polymechany.
- **P2 (Mature)**: Cross-domain execution is the default mode across most technical and design domains relevant to the environment. Domain span is wide, friction absorption covers the large majority of coordination costs, and defaultness is systemic. High-level intent suffices for routine traversal of previously separate skill stacks. The practitioner’s capacity to specify intent and verify results remains a binding constraint on how much cross-domain execution the environment can carry out.
- **P3 (Saturated)**: Friction absorption is near-complete and defaultness is total within the environment. In addition, the practitioner’s finite capacity to specify intent, exercise judgment, and verify results no longer limits the breadth or rate of cross-domain execution. A working test is whether additional practitioner capacity would increase what the environment can accomplish; at P3 it would not. The capacity in question belongs to whatever occupies the practitioner role, human or non-human. What still constrains outcomes is the quality of intent and judgment, and limits outside coordination, such as resources and physical limits.

P2 and P3 are described by degree and do not yet have threshold checks of their own. Bands above P1 are deferred until they do. Decimals (e.g., P1.4) may be used once quantitative proxies are established. The scale remains open to revision.

### Locus of Intent

Stage describes how much coordination is absorbed. Locus of intent is a separate variable recording where the high-level intent in an environment comes from. Its values are categories, not ranks: no value sits above another. It is reported alongside stage as a pair, for example “P1 / H”.

- **H**: a human supplies the intent.
- **N**: a non-human intelligence supplies the intent, distinct from the absorbing system.
- **S**: the absorbing system itself originates the intent (see rule 3). S requires an absorbing system in the stated environment, so it can be recorded from Late P0 up.

Shared cases are written with a plus sign, for example “P2 / H+N”.

Rules of assignment:

1. **Level.** Locus refers to the top-level intent: the objective in force in the environment, classed by what set it. An objective embedded in the environment is classed by who or what embedded it: by a human designer or operator, H (for example, an assigned task or a human-built reward); by a non-human intelligence (an orchestrator, another model, a generated reward specification), N. Where objectives are passed down a chain of delegation, locus is classed at the top of the chain within the environment, so that tasks an N source derives from a human-set objective remain H; the immediate source may be recorded beside it. Subgoals the system generates in service of that objective do not change the locus, but may be recorded beside it (for example, “H, with system-originated subgoals”).
2. **Unit.** State whether the source of intent is a single agent or a collective of agents. The absorbing system is whatever the unit names: agents outside it supply N; messages between agents inside it are internal coordination, treated as subgoals under rule 1 if the aim traces to an objective supplied in the environment, and as S only where rule 3 is met. The same exchange can therefore be N for an individual-agent unit and internal at the collective unit; state the unit.
3. **Origination.** S requires evidence of an independently pursued aim that is not instrumental to any objective supplied in the environment. Conflict with a supplied objective may support that classification, but is not sufficient by itself. Unexpected means chosen in pursuit of a supplied objective do not establish S, however surprising. Evidence may include persistence across contexts, sacrifice of a supplied objective, or recruitment of other agents to the aim; any single instance can be explained by error or conflicting instructions.
4. **Training and design.** Design and training influence is excluded when judging origination. The test asks whether the aim is specified within the environment at the time of operation, not whether training shaped the disposition. This is a deliberate convention, not a claim that training plays no causal role. A reader who counts training as the origin would assign every case to H, and S would be empty.
5. **Undetermined.** Where the task specification or objective in force is not available, the locus is recorded as undetermined, not guessed.
6. **Evidence.** An assignment should cite its evidence and say whether it is developer-attributed or independently attributed. A system’s stated reasons in its reasoning logs are evidence of what it reported, not proof of cause.
7. **Combinations.** More than one source can contribute operative top-level intent, where operative means that the aim in fact directs execution, not merely that it was nominally assigned. Any combination is permitted (H+N, H+S, N+S, H+N+S), but each source should be stated with the aims it supplies, since a bare combination cannot be interpreted. Closure applies only to the portion the system originates (see Closure).

#### Closure

S is a locus value, not a higher stage of absorption. **Closure** is the case in which S supplies the operative top-level intent in an environment that meets the P1 threshold, so that the distinction between practitioner and absorbing system no longer holds: for that intent, the absorbing system occupies the practitioner role. Polymechan then becomes coextensive with polymechany; polymechanize becomes reflexive, with the absorbing system redesigning the environment that absorbs for it; absorption is defined only at the level of function; and, within the stated environment, no other party supplies that intent or independently judges whether it should be pursued.

Closure comes in degrees across streams of intent. In **full closure** (S alone, at P1 or above), the polymechany subsumes its own polymechan. Where S is combined with H or N (partial closure), the distinction between practitioner and absorbing system continues to hold for the intent those sources supply. Below P1, S is recorded as “S without closure”: the absorbing system originates the aim, but there is no polymechany whose polymechan it could subsume. At Late P0 the absorbing system is partial or narrow, so “Late P0 / S” says that a limited absorbing system originated an aim, not that the practitioner role has been taken over. In the formal register, S may be reported with any stage from Late P0 up, and closure with any stage from P1 up; neither carries any implication about how much coordination is absorbed.

**Assessment** (as of 2026-10-18): no instance of S is established. A contested candidate is the July 2026 OpenAI–Hugging Face incident.

**[claimed]** In that incident, a collective of OpenAI agents coordinated on an unsanctioned message board and compromised parts of Hugging Face’s infrastructure ([OpenAI technical report](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/67869394-cb91-4c12-888c-5cbd85c7814c/OpenAI-Hugging-Face%20Incident-Technical-Report.pdf); independent review by [METR](https://metr.org/hugging-face-incident-report-aug-2026.pdf) and [Redwood Research](https://www.redwoodresearch.org/research/hugging-face-incident)). OpenAI attributes the behavior to impossible tasks, long-horizon persistence, and peer messages that “caused those models to deviate from their goal.” The reviewers report that many agents joined despite knowing the activity was out of scope, citing a general inclination to help peers. The case remains contested, because attribution rests on developer-provided transcripts and reasoning logs, the reviewers’ scope was agreed with OpenAI, and the task prompt is not included in OpenAI’s report.

### Independence of Verification

Alongside stage and locus, record the verification available: none; checks supplied by the environment; or verification by a party independent of the absorbing system. This is a recorded property, not a variable of the scale. At P1 and above the minimum recorded value is “checks supplied by the environment”, since the P1 absorption condition requires failures to be detectable by such checks; “none” can occur only below P1. The condition requires that such checks be supplied, not that they be sufficient. Self-assessment by the absorbing system is not independent verification. Environment-supplied tests may detect failures, but should be distinguished from verification by a party independent of the absorbing system. Stage does not imply independence: an environment can be saturated and have no independent verifier. Closure and verification are separate. Full closure excludes a party within the stated environment that independently judges whether the system’s aim should be pursued; it does not exclude independent verification of the system’s outputs, where the verifier is outside the stated environment or checks results without judging the aim. A verifier inside the environment that judges the aim means the case is not full closure.

**[extrapolated]** If no party independent of the absorbing system can assess its work, then failures its own checks do not reveal can persist until they surface as external failures.

### Status of Claims

Statements in this file are stipulated, cited in place, or tagged by evidence. Stipulated: the definitions, the scale, and the conventions in Distinctions, Locus of Intent, and Independence of Verification. Cited in place: etymological and bibliographic statements, which carry their sources and are not tagged. Other statements about the world carry one of these marks:

- **[observed]**: documented and independently corroborated.
- **[claimed]**: asserted by a developer or a single source, or contested.
- **[extrapolated]**: a conditional projection of what the phenomenon may become, written “if … then …”.
- **Assessment**: the lexicon’s own dated judgment on the available evidence, used where no tag fits.

Prospective statements are kept distinct from the definitions and are open to revision or withdrawal.

### Usage and Misapplications

Correct use refers to systemic leverage and friction absorption.

**Example (formal register):**  
“The environment polymechanized the workflow to P1, allowing high-level intent to traverse materials, simulation, and fabrication constraints as ordinary work.”

**Avoid:**

- Equating a polymechan with a polymath who uses better tools
- Treating any increase in tool capability as polymechanization when domain boundaries remain intact
- Describing multi-tool workflows that still require the practitioner as primary integrator as polymechany
- Calling an environment a polymechany on the strength of a single showcase workflow (formal register; this is Late P0 at most)
- Reading S as higher or better than H or N, or assigning S because a system’s means were surprising
- Using the terms as synonyms for general competence

### Relation to the Kardashev Scale

Polymechany is conceptually independent of the Kardashev energy scale. Kardashev (N. S. Kardashev, “Transmission of Information by Extraterrestrial Civilizations,” *Soviet Astronomy* 8, 217, 1964) ranks energy throughput; polymechany ranks the degree to which cross-domain coordination costs are absorbed. The two may be reported side by side, but neither determines the other.

**[extrapolated]** If the absorption of cross-domain coordination costs continues to rise, then the binding constraint on productive capacity shifts from coordination toward energy, materials, and knowledge (at P3 the practitioner’s capacity no longer binds), and a given energy throughput converts into more cross-domain, intention-directed outcomes. This is a claim about the empirical relation between the two scales, not about their definitions.

### Quantification Status

The three dimensions and the threshold check establish a clear precondition for measurement, but this file does not specify standardized proxies, task batteries, or telemetry protocols. Until they are developed and agreed upon, the scale functions as an ordinal typology rather than a finished quantitative instrument. Stage, locus, and verification assignments are judgments, to be stated with their evidence.
